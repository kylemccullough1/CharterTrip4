# MURDER AT BRAUN MANOR
### A phone-driven murder mystery for 25 — the rundown

---

## The pitch

James Braun — industrialist, collector, and a very frightened man — throws a party. Every guest gets a personal invitation letter before the night explaining who they are and why Braun wants them there. Fifteen minutes into the party, the lights die, lightning cracks, a scream — and Braun is gone. Blood on the study floor, no body, and a house full of people who all had a reason.

Everything runs on phones. Everyone plays a character. Three of the guests did it. Six people will be convicted over three trials. Whether the right ones go down is entirely up to the room.

It's Mafia meets a dinner-party mystery, except nobody sits in a circle — you work the house, trade what you saw, and lie to your friends' faces.

## The one-minute version of playing it

Before the party you get a text: a letter from James Braun, inviting your character. You upload a photo, pick your name. At the party, your phone holds who you are, what you want, what you're hiding, and what you saw tonight. A name tag with a QR code — scan people as you meet them, it's the party's mingle game.

When the murder hits, your phone updates: you get a secret role. Nine clue cards are hidden through the house as QR codes — scan one and it publishes to the big screen for everyone. The screen shows the house map, every clue found, every guest's face, and who's been eliminated.

Three trials. Each one convicts two people. Talk, accuse, defend, vote — all on your phone, results on the big screen. At the end of the night, the full story plays: who did it, how, and the winners — including a few people who won an hour earlier and never said a word.

## The factions (21 players)

**The Syndicate's Hand — 3 killers.** Braun was about to expose the Midnight Syndicate, a criminal organization marked by a snail. Three guests killed him: one had *access* to the study, one had the *means*, one carries the snail and gave the *order*. They know each other, they have rehearsed cover stories, and they win if even one of them survives the night.

**Syndicate Associates — 2 minions.** They know who the killers are. They didn't touch anyone. They win if the killers win, and they hold one nasty trick: they can make a convicted killer look innocent on the reveal — or make one of their own look like a killer, selling the room a fake victory.

**The Investigators — 3 detectives.** Hidden among the guests, they know each other. They can pull another player's phone data (the target finds out *someone* looked), run forensics on clues to detect tampering, and — late in the game — ask the one question that never lies: is this person a killer.

**The Desperate — 2 jesters.** Ruin is coming for them either way. They want to be convicted, and they can tamper with clues to frame *themselves*. If the room votes one out, they win — silently. Nobody finds out until the end.

**The Braun Claim — 2.** Harry (the estranged brother) and Isla (the fiancée). They don't care who killed James. Each wins only if the *other* is convicted and disgraced while they survive. They know exactly who their rival is.

**Guests of the House — 9 villagers.** They saw things. Real things. They win if all three killers are caught.

Jester and Braun wins never end the game and are never announced — those players are quietly folded into the winners at the final reveal.

## The night (about 2 hours)

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| The Party | 15 min | Mingle in character. No one has a role. Braun works the room. |
| **The Murder** | 3 min | Lights out. Screen flash, thunder, the scream. Clues get planted. Roles hit every phone. |
| Investigation | 30 min | Find clues, trade what you saw, catch people lying. |
| **Trial 1** | ~15 min | Vote → top 4 defend themselves → 2 convicted → their card revealed. |
| Discussion | 10 min | The screen announces if a clue has been tampered with. Never which. |
| **Trial 2** | ~15 min | Two more. |
| Last Hours | 10 min | The heavy abilities come online. |
| **Trial 3** | ~15 min | The last two. |
| **The Reveal** | 10 min | The full reconstruction, every faction, every secret winner. |

Convicted players become **ghosts**: they can't vote or spill secrets, but they can send haunted emoji messages to the living, trigger one thunderclap on somebody's phone, and heckle trials anonymously on the big screen.

## Why it won't feel like standing around

Every player's phone quietly coaches them. The badge scans tell the system who's been left out of conversations, who's holding information nobody has heard, and who's been told two contradicting stories — and it nudges people accordingly: *"You were in the cellar. So was someone else. Find out what they saw."* Nobody gets a script, and nobody gets stranded.

Three of us play staff characters — the chef, the designer, the butler — with no stake in the game. Their whole job is keeping the room moving, feeding hints to stuck players, and making sure the quiet ones get pulled in. The fourth plays Braun, dies gloriously, and runs the game from the back room.

## The replayability trick

Nothing is fixed. The system deals the game fresh every time: who stands where in the house, which three guests are the killers, which innocent people look suspicious anyway, and which clues appear in which rooms. Same characters, different murder. We've simulated thousands of deals to make sure every version is solvable and no character is "always the killer."
